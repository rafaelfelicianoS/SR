\section{System Description and Design Principles}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{Scope, Users and Interfaces}

SecureBank~PT provides six functions: device enrolment and login, SEPA
and instant transfers, MB~WAY payments, card management (freezing,
spending limits and \ac{PIN} change), account statements and transaction
history, and back-office user and account management.
Figure~\ref{fig:context} shows the context diagram. The \emph{Customer}
uses the mobile application on Android or iOS, and the \emph{Admin}, who
operates the application and checks that everything in it runs
correctly, uses the back-office desktop application. The Admin role
covers several functions, such as operation and audit, each with its own
permissions. The system has three external
interfaces: the core banking system through an internal REST~\ac{API},
the MB~WAY network, and the Multibanco/SIBS national payment scheme,
represented together as the \emph{Bank}. All clients are untrusted and
reach the bank through an \ac{API} gateway with TLS~1.3 and certificate
pinning. The desktop also connects through a VPN with \ac{mTLS}, and keys
are stored in an \ac{HSM}.

\subsection{Security Requirements}

The following requirements were defined in a team workshop.
\textbf{R1}~\ac{RBAC} for customers and admins, where the user
explicitly confirms the role in use. \textbf{R2}~\ac{MFA} combining
biometrics (inherence) and a six-digit \ac{PIN} (knowledge) on an enrolled
device (possession), which satisfies \ac{PSD2}
\ac{SCA}~\cite{psd2rts}. \textbf{R3}~No cached login: the
session ends when the application is closed or after two minutes of
inactivity, and tokens are short-lived. \textbf{R4}~Authentication
depends on a per-user key kept in the secure element of the device. \textbf{R5}~A matrix card code and the \ac{PIN} are used as
a recovery factor when the device is lost. \textbf{R6}~Isolated channels
per user and per operation, rejecting requests under overload instead of
degrading isolation. \textbf{R7}~Atomic operations and a \ac{BtG} protocol
for exceptional admin access. \textbf{R8}~Encryption of data in transit
and at rest. \textbf{R9}~Logging of every authentication attempt, with rate
limiting and brute-force alerts sent to the \ac{SIEM} for investigation.

\subsection{Assessment Against Design Principles}

The initial design was reviewed by the team against ten secure design
principles, the eight of Saltzer and Schroeder~\cite{saltzer1975} plus
defence in depth and fail securely. Table~\ref{tab:principles} summarises
the result.

\begin{table}[t]
\caption{Design principles in the initial design: honoured (\checkmark) or violated (\texttimes)}
\label{tab:principles}
\centering
\footnotesize
\begin{tabularx}{\columnwidth}{@{}L{2.45cm}@{\,}c@{\,}Y@{}}
\toprule
\textbf{Principle} & & \textbf{Evidence in the design} \\
\midrule
Least privilege & \checkmark & Users cannot manage users; admins cannot move money \\
Fail-safe defaults & \checkmark & Failed login or transfer is denied \\
Economy of mech. & \checkmark & One permission manager for app and desktop \\
Complete mediation & \texttimes & Login cached, no check on each access \\
Open design & \checkmark & Knowing the architecture does not reveal the \ac{PIN} \\
Separation of priv. & \checkmark & \ac{MFA} for large transfers; second admin to freeze accounts \\
Least common mech. & \texttimes & Single global server for all data \\
Psych. accept. & \texttimes & Users reject frequent \ac{MFA}, logins and expiring tokens \\
Defence in depth & \checkmark & \ac{PIN}, \ac{MFA}, expiring tokens, bank matrix card \\
Fail securely & \checkmark & Errors show a generic message, no technical details \\
\bottomrule
\end{tabularx}
\end{table}

The three violations are mitigated in the final design. For complete
mediation, the cached login is replaced by R3, and \ac{MFA} is required
for critical actions such as large transfers. For least common mechanism,
critical data is kept in separate components, internal services are
isolated, and sensitive data is stored in independent regions. For
psychological acceptability, biometrics and a six-digit \ac{PIN} replace
complex passwords, tokens are renewed silently while the user is active,
and step-up \ac{MFA} is only requested when needed.

\section{Cyber Resilience Act Quick-Scan}
\label{sec:cra}

This section checks whether SecureBank~PT is in scope of the \ac{CRA}
(Regulation (EU) 2024/2847)~\cite{cra}, which obligations apply and when.

\subsection{Scope and Classification}
The \ac{CRA} applies to products with digital elements whose intended use
includes a data connection to a device or network (Article~2). The mobile
application is software distributed to consumers through the app stores
as part of the commercial activity of the bank, so it is in scope and the
bank is its manufacturer. The fact that the application is free does not exclude it from scope. The
\ac{API} gateway and the mobile backend are needed for the application to
work, so they count as its remote data processing and are also covered.
The core banking system and the SIBS network are not part of the product.
They are ICT systems of a financial entity and are governed by
\ac{DORA}~\cite{dora} and \ac{NIS2}.

The application is not listed in Annex~III or Annex~IV. Although it
authenticates users, it is not an identity management product, so it
falls in the default category. Therefore, conformity can be shown by
internal control (Module~A) without a notified body, but the essential
requirements of Annex~I apply in full.

\subsection{Applicable Obligations}
Part~I of Annex~I covers the properties of the product. SecureBank~PT is
released without known exploitable vulnerabilities and secure by default.
Unauthorised access is prevented by R1--R4, data is encrypted in transit
and at rest (R8), and integrity is protected by signed operations,
certificate pinning and root and tamper detection. Debug interfaces and
unused libraries are removed from release builds to reduce the attack
surface, and security events are logged (R9).

Part~II covers vulnerability handling. Each build produces a signed
CycloneDX \ac{SBOM}, the bank publishes a coordinated vulnerability
disclosure policy through \texttt{security.txt}, and security fixes are
free and released separately from feature updates whenever possible. The
support period is five years, the minimum set by Article~13 unless the
product is expected to be used for less time. The bank must also keep the technical
documentation, sign the EU declaration of conformity and affix the CE
marking.

\subsection{Timeline and Reporting}
The reporting obligations of Article~14 apply since 11~September~2026, so
they already bind the bank. An actively exploited vulnerability or a
severe incident requires an early warning within 24~hours, a notification
within 72~hours and a final report, submitted through the \ac{ENISA}
single reporting platform to the national \ac{CSIRT} acting as
coordinator, which in Portugal is CERT.PT within the \ac{CNCS}. The
remaining obligations apply from 11~December~2027, after which no release
can be placed on the market without the conformity assessment and CE
marking. A severe incident in the backend may also be a major ICT incident
under \ac{DORA}, reported to Banco de Portugal, so the \ac{PSIRT} and the
CO keep a single \ac{IRP} with both sets of deadlines.

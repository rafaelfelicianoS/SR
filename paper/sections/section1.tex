\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

Mobile banking is the main channel between Portuguese retail banks and
their customers. The application runs on untrusted consumer devices and
communicates directly with the core banking systems, so a flaw in its
design or implementation exposes the money and personal data of the
customers. Even so, security is still often treated as an afterthought.
Developers often neglect it for lack of resources and priority~\cite{assal2018soups},
and many vulnerabilities come from predictable human errors~\cite{anu2020human}.

Past failures rarely needed a sophisticated attack. In 2017, Equifax
exposed the data of about 147 million people through an Apache Struts flaw
patched two months earlier, because it had no inventory of where the
component ran and no working patch process~\cite{equifax2018}. In 2012, Knight Capital
lost about 440 million dollars in 45 minutes after a deployment reached
only seven of eight servers~\cite{knight2013}. Both were lifecycle
failures, which shows that security activities must be planned, assigned
and verified.

\subsection{Security Context}

Mobile banking in the European Union is regulated by the \ac{PSD2}~\cite{psd2},
which mandates \ac{SCA} for payments, by the \ac{DORA}~\cite{dora}, which
requires financial entities to demonstrate operational resilience and
incident response capability, and by the \ac{GDPR}~\cite{gdpr}. The main
threats are credential stuffing with passwords leaked from other services,
phishing through fake websites, SMS or phone calls, man-in-the-middle
attacks on the communication with the bank, and abuse of the backend
\acp{API} to obtain personal data or execute privileged actions. Fraudulent
transfers are direct losses for the bank, and each incident damages its
reputation. Moreover, the \ac{CRA}~\cite{cra} now requires manufacturers of
digital products to handle security during the whole lifecycle, to keep a
\ac{SBOM}, to ship products secure by default and to report actively
exploited vulnerabilities.

\subsection{Approach}

This paper plans a \ac{SSDL} for SecureBank~PT, the mobile application of a
Portuguese retail bank. The plan follows the NIST \ac{SSDF}
(SP~800-218)~\cite{nist800218} and uses OWASP \ac{SAMM}~v2~\cite{samm} to
measure maturity. The design binds authentication to the device with
biometrics and a six-digit \ac{PIN} instead of SMS \acp{OTP}, keeps
sessions short, signs every sensitive operation and executes transfers
as atomic and idempotent operations. Section~\ref{sec:system} describes
the system and its design principles, Section~\ref{sec:lifecycle} the
lifecycle plan, Section~\ref{sec:cra} the \ac{CRA} quick-scan and
Section~\ref{sec:ai} the use of AI tools. Section~\ref{sec:conclusion}
concludes the paper.
